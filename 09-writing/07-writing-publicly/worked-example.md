# The same claim, above and below the floor

## Below the floor

> Connection pools are important because opening a connection is expensive. If
> your pool is too small, requests queue up and latency increases. You should
> size your pool based on your workload.

Every sentence is true. Every sentence is also free, infinite, and available one
prompt away, which means the post competes with an unbounded supply of the same
thing and lands accordingly. Nothing in it required a machine.

## Above it

> On `<machine, OS, arch>` with `<Postgres version>` and `<driver version>`, a
> pool of `<n>` with `max_overflow=0` under an open-loop load of `<rate>` req/s
> produced `<your measured p99>` while `<n2>` produced `<your measured p99>`.
> Here is the k6 script and the compose file. The part I got wrong first: I
> measured with a fixed-VU generator, which self-throttles when the service slows,
> and so it could not reproduce queueing at all — the numbers above are from the
> rerun.

Three things are happening. The **conditions** are stated, so the result is
checkable. The **artifacts** are attached, so it is reproducible. And the
**mistake** is in the body rather than omitted, which is the part that could not
have been generated by anything that did not make it.

That last sentence invites a reader to reply "your driver version has a known
regression in that path". That is not a risk to manage. It is the entire return
on publishing.

## Your material, ranked by scarcity

Not by topic, and not by how finished it feels. By the question: *what is in here
that could not have been generated without access to my machine, my production
system, or my mistakes?*

**1. The sanitised latency postmortem** from
[Topic 4](../04-the-postmortem/README.md). Highest value and hardest. Real
incident narratives with real numbers are among the rarest things on the
technical internet, and the detection-gap section — what you believed first, and
why that was reasonable — is the part nobody publishes. Sanitisation gate first,
employer sign-off in writing.

**2. The negative result this lab already contains.** Layer 1's results tables
were published without being run, and its C++/Rust "no lost updates" figure turned
out to be the optimiser hoisting the increment out of the loop; rebuilt at `-O0`,
lost updates appear in the millions. That claim and its provenance are recorded in
this lab's [root README](../../README.md) and
[`PREDICTIONS.md`](../../PREDICTIONS.md) — **and it is not yours until you rerun
it.** Publish *your* number, from *your* machine, with *your* compiler version
and the exact build flags. A post that repeats this lab's figure has borrowed
someone else's measurement, which is the specific failure the whole story is
about.

**3. Anything where the docs said one thing and your machine did another.** Layer
1's macOS failures qualify with a reproducible attachment: code written against
`<sys/epoll.h>` and `/proc` does not build or run on Darwin at all. The post is
not "epoll is Linux-only" — everybody knows that. It is "here is what the failure
actually looks like, and here is the kqueue path that replaces it."

**4. A prediction you logged and got wrong.** `PREDICTIONS.md` is a running record
of your own model being wrong, in public, with dates. That is the same asset as
the negative result in a smaller package, and it comes with a timestamp proving
you wrote the prediction first.

## The cadence, made mechanical

The last working day of the month, publish whatever is closest to done. The
month's lab work is the raw material, so the pipeline is already full; the
deadline is what converts a folder of drafts into published work. One post a
month you keep beats a weekly cadence you abandon in March, and the compounding
does not start until there is a body of work to compound.

## Distribution is a different problem from writing

Send it directly to three engineers you know and ask: *"is there anything here
you would push back on?"* That is also the measurement in this topic's record
table — and it is the only thing that disambiguates silence, which otherwise
means either "nobody read it" or "there was nothing to argue with", two problems
with opposite fixes.
